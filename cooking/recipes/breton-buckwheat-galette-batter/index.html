Breton Buckwheat Galette Batter

The authentic 100% buckwheat galette batter from a Breton master crêpier - flour, water, salt and a spoon of honey, rested 24 to 48 hours

SERVINGS: 15
PREP: 20 MIN
COOK: 20 MIN
medium
CUISINE: French

Ingredients

For about 15 galettes on a 40 cm griddle:

  • 500g buckwheat flour (blé noir), ideally IGP Bretagne
  • 1L water at room temperature — 500ml on day 1 + 500ml on day 2
  • 20g coarse Guérande sea salt
  • 1 tbsp honey (optional, for colour and for the kraz — the crisp edge)
  • Lard for greasing the griddle (or coconut oil for a vegan version)

That is the whole list. No egg, no wheat flour, no beer, no milk, no fat in the batter — those are shortcuts for same-day cooking, not the traditional Breton recipe.

Instructions

DAY 1: Mix the batter

  1. Start at least 24 hours before you plan to eat, ideally 48 hours for deeper flavour and a browner galette.
  2. In a large mixing bowl, combine the flour, salt, honey and 500ml of the water.
  3. Mix by hand, hooking your fingers into a claw. Never use a stand mixer — it breaks the fibres of the flour.
  4. When the batter turns firm, open your hand and slap the batter with your palm for about 2 minutes to aerate it and make it supple.
  5. Cover the bowl with cling film and refrigerate.

DAY 2 (or 3): Loosen the batter

  1. Take the batter out of the refrigerator about 1 hour before cooking.
  2. Whisk in the remaining 500ml of water, by hand or with a whisk, until smooth.

Cook the galettes

  1. Heat the griddle to about 200°C. The traditional Breton appliance is a bilig — a cast-iron plate heated by electricity or gas. It is what gives thin, crisp galettes (kraz in Breton).
  2. Spread a thin layer of lard (or coconut oil) so the batter does not stick.
  3. Pour on one ladle of batter and spread it with the rozell (the wooden rake). Cook for about 1 minute.
  4. Either lift the galette off for later use, or garnish it right away with your chosen filling.

Notes

Why does the batter have to rest?

Resting for several hours removes acidity, brings out the flavour and makes the galette easier to digest. Two days in the refrigerator is best: the galettes take on a lovely brown colour instead of staying pale, and they taste far better.

Buckwheat or blé noir?

Two names for the same thing. Several French regions grow it; Brittany is the reference, and the Harpe Noire variety gives an excellent flavour. China, Canada and Russia are the biggest world producers — much of the imported grain is less good, so check that the buckwheat was actually grown in Brittany.

Why do my galettes break?

  • Not enough fermentation — rest the batter at least 24 hours.
  • Batter spread too thick — it must be as fine as lace, or it tears when turned.
  • Mixed with a machine instead of by hand, breaking the fibres.
  • No honey — a spoonful gives colour and the kraz.
  • Egg, wheat flour, beer or another ferment in the batter.
  • Griddle too cool — it should be around 200°C.

Why does the batter stick to the griddle?

If the batter boils and sticks to the plate, the seasoning (culottage) is insufficient or badly done: keep building up layers of fat while heating the bilig to 270°C. A plate that is too badly burnt needs a complete re-seasoning. If the batter only boils, it is too thin — add a little more buckwheat flour.

Adjusting hydration

Not every mill works the same way, and the origin of the buckwheat matters too. If the batter stays runny and gluey after day 1 and you cannot slap it, add flour. Next time start with less water and note the quantity that suits your flour. Cooking in a frying pan rather than on a bilig needs a thinner batter, so add a little extra water.

Filling ideas

  • La complète: egg + Emmental + ham + salted butter.
  • La lyonnaise: with cooking saucisson from Lyon.
  • Galette-saucisse: the Rennes speciality, with a soubise sauce made from Roscoff onions.
  • Serve with a glass of dry cider.

Allergens

None, unless indicated on the products you buy.
